# Moonshot Pinball: a playtest review from an itch / r/indiegaming regular

**What I actually played:** about 10 runs' worth of title screens on a 390×844 phone viewport. I played Challenges 1–4 for real with my own swing timing (a reflex bot, plus a deliberately sloppy version with ±100–180ms timing error to fake a normal thumb). I also did one 3-strike wipeout on purpose to see the fail screens. That run reached Inning 2 and the Upper Deck legitimately. In a second run I **cheated** (set the score to the target) through C1–C4 only to see different drafts and Manage Machine, then played C5 on the Upper Deck myself. I can't hear anything in this setup, so I have no opinion on sound beyond "Credits says it's synthesized."

---

## 1. First 30 seconds

Title screen: clean. "MOONSHOT / PINBALL" in a chunky italic condensed face, a gold NEW RUN button, the table dimmed behind it. It looks like a real product, not a game jam entry. I tapped New Run and got "INNING 1 · STARTER · CHALLENGE 1 OF 15, TARGET 1,500" and PLAY BALL. Two taps and I'm playing. Good.

Then the first pitch comes straight down the middle and drains in about one second because I didn't swing in time. "BALL DRAINED, Strike 1, +0 banked." Fine, that's on me. Next pitch I connect, the ball rockets into the baseball-shaped bumpers, and the score ticks to 1,500 in about 20 seconds. **Cleared on the first ball.**

Would I keep playing? **Yes, for a few more challenges.** It looks good and "baseball bat as the flipper" is a real hook I haven't seen. But my gut at 30 seconds is "huh, that was easy, and I mostly watched." The hook has to land by challenge 3 or I bounce.

## 2. Look

**Pro:**
- The HUD frame is good: THIS BALL / SCORE / TARGET / a stadium-scoreboard MULTIPLIER box, gold trim, and a crowd-bokeh background. It reads like a polished mobile game.
- The table art is cohesive: big baseball pop bumpers, a catcher's-mitt bumper, a pitcher's mound in the middle, an infield-dirt arc, foul lines, and home plate with batter's boxes at the bottom. Bumper hits get an orange glow, a ring and "+100" floaters. Nice.
- The Upper Deck reveal is actually cool. The table gets taller, a second bumper field appears above the JACKPOT pennant, and a translucent HOME RUN ramp curls up the right side with chevrons that light up as the ball rides it.

**Placeholder-ish or weak:**
- **The ball is tiny on a phone.** It's a little white dot with a cyan streak. It's fine on the base table, but once the Upper Deck zooms the camera out it's a speck. For a game where everything is timing, the ball should be the most readable thing on screen.
- **The batter is tiny too.** It's a little red-helmet chibi the size of a thumbnail, and it sits right on top of the blue slingshot art when you slide to the edge.
- **Bought bumpers look like an afterthought.** "Triple Bumper Cluster: BUNDLE: three pop bumpers" turns out to be three small magenta dots wedged near the right wall. The factory bumpers are big chunky baseballs, and the thing I picked as my exciting upgrade looks like a sprinkle. The same goes for Twin Bumpers.
- Letterboxing: at 390×844 there's an empty navy band of about 60px above the HUD and about 140px below the table. On a phone that's real estate you could give to the ball.

## 3. Feel

- **Batting:** this is the hook, and it mostly works. Tap the right half, the bat whips through, and early/on-time/late sends the ball left/center/right. A clean center hit sends it screaming up the middle with a satisfying streak. It's genuinely more interesting than flippers *in concept*.
- **But it's very forgiving.** By my count the contact window on a centered pitch is roughly 0.25–0.3s, and I had to swing *way* early before I'd miss. My deliberately sloppy-timing runs still cleared Challenge 1 on the first ball in 6 of 6 tries, with 2–9 swings. Generous is fine for a phone game, but right now skill barely matters early on.
- **The pitcher is a pitching machine.** Every opening pitch I saw from C1 through C4 was identical: straight down the middle at the same speed, and the batter starts pre-lined-up for it. No curve, no change-up, no inside/outside. In a *baseball* game the pitch is the content! Right now the only "pitching" is the ball falling off bumpers.
- **You watch more than you play.** A good hit sends the ball rattling around the upper bumpers for 15–40 seconds with no input from me. Real pinball has that too, but here the flipper-equivalent is one tiny bat at the bottom, so long stretches are just watching numbers go up. My balls lasted 20–50 seconds and needed 10–47 swings across the whole ball.
- **Juice:** bumper flashes, glow rings, the multiplier box pulsing orange when it ticks up, and +3000 on the Home Run ramp all land OK. What's missing is impact *on the bat hit itself*: no hit-stop, no screen shake, no "CRACK" text, at least none I could catch in screenshots. The bat hit is the core verb, and it should feel like the loudest thing in the game.
- **Challenges end mid-rally.** The moment you hit the target the ball is yanked off the table and "CLEARED!" pops up. I get why, but it kills any "watch the number go stupid" fantasy. In Peglin and Ball x Pit, overkill is half the fun.

## 4. Upgrades / roguelite layer

**What's good:**
- **Tap-to-preview is excellent.** Tap a card and the table highlights *exactly* which bumper it affects, with a dashed ring and a label ("Hall of Fame: Every 5 hits on Left bumper: +0.2×"). That's the single best piece of UX here. Balatro-likes usually make you guess.
- **Machines vs. Boosts and the Power budget (⚡4/7) is a smart structure.** Physical parts that take up a "slot," plus free modifiers, give you a real build-space concept. Pick-a-spot mounting ("Left alley top / Right alley top") is cool too: you're literally building the table.
- Some machines are spicy: Turbo Ramp ("8 checkpoints × 1,000"), Glove Scoop ("catches the ball for +1,000 and relaunches it into the upper bumpers"), Multiball Splitter, Multiplier Gate.

**What's not:**
- **The boosts are generic filler with baseball names.** Rally Cap = "All bumpers ×1.25." MVP = "×2 on Top-left bumper." Big Stage = "×2 on Jackpot." Hot Corner = "+100 on Glove bumper." Crowd Noise / Standing Ovation = "every 20 bumper hits +0.1/0.2×." These are the same "+flat / ×mult on thing X" every Balatro clone has. The names are cute, but nothing interacts with *batting*: no "bunts do X," no "pulled hits score double," no "a 3-hit streak = extra base." **Hall of Fame** for "every 5 hits on left bumper" doesn't even make sense as a name.
- **Little sense of synergy.** Most boosts point at one specific bumper. With so few parts, I never felt a build "come online." My multiplier sat at **x1.0** for nearly the whole run, and the biggest I saw was x1.4. Meanwhile the MULTIPLIER box is the most visually important thing in the HUD. It's like a slot machine with the jackpot light permanently off.
- **A dead draft:** at the C4 free pick, two of the three offers were machines that "Needs room: needs 1 more free Power" (Glove Scoop, Turbo Ramp). So my "choose 1 of 3" was really "take Standing Ovation." Either don't offer what I can't fit, or let me sell inline.
- **The shop only ever had boosts** in every visit I saw. Machines only came from free picks. It's a 4-card shop with $1 reroll and nothing to plan around.
- Repeats: Standing Ovation and Bumper Crew showed up in nearly every draft.

Would I know what to pick? Yes, the clarity is great. Would I *care* which I picked? Not much yet.

## 5. The baseball theme

It's more than a skin, but less than it could be.

**Where it matters:** the bat replaces the flippers, swing timing aims your shot, strikes are lives, there are innings with Starter/Reliever/Closer tiers, a HOME RUN ramp, and the Upper Deck. That's a real mechanical translation, and the batting-as-flipper idea is the reason this game exists.

**Where it's a skin:** baseballs as bumpers, a glove as a bumper, and baseball-named boosts that are just point multipliers. There's no pitch variety, no counts/balls, no baserunners, no "on base" state, no RBIs. The Starter/Reliever/Closer label is literally "challenge 1/2/3 of the inning," just a bigger number. Every pitch is the same meatball. Baseball has so much juicy structure (runners on base as a combo meter, pitch types as enemy behavior, a Closer with a nasty pitch as a boss) and almost none of it is used.

## 6. Onboarding

- **Mostly the right amount, well done.** The How to Play is short and readable (Move & Swing, Aim with Timing, The Goal, Build Your Machine, Multiplier, The Run, Saving). The first pitch shows big dashed "DRAG / LEFT THUMB · MOVE" and "TAP / RIGHT THUMB · SWING" zones that fade once you play. Tips are one-time and dismissable (GOT IT), and Settings has "Show tips again." Respect.
- **Issues:**
  - The "Nice hit! Swing early to pull it left, late to push it right." banner appears *during live play* and sits over the top row of bumpers for several seconds. That's where the ball is.
  - "Left thumb: drag to move · Right thumb: tap to swing" is printed on **every** modal, forever: the cleared screen, the strike screen, pause, even **GAME OVER**. By challenge 2 it's noise.
  - The POWER ⚡ tip on the first draft pushes the third card down. It's fine, but the draft panel is cramped on a phone. The bottom "Machine: factory table" line is clipped.

## 7. Bugs / jank

- The **"HOME RUN! RALLY ×2"** callout renders right on top of the "UPPER DECK" floor label at the top of the table, so the two texts overlap.
- **The draft offers machines you can't afford in Power.** That's not a crash, but 2 of 3 dead cards is a bug in spirit.
- When the batter is moved to the far left, the sprite overlaps the left slingshot art.
- The pause button still shows **II** on the GAME OVER screen.
- GAME OVER is barebones: "0 of 1,500 on challenge 1. Machine: factory table." There are no run stats, no best score and no "you reached inning X." For a roguelite, the death screen is where you hook the "one more run."
- Credits: the "Version 1.0.0 (b3422d0)" line isn't aligned with the label/value grid above it. Nitpick.
- No console errors or crashes in about 10 sessions. Save/continue and pause worked.

## 8. Comparison

- **vs Peglin:** Peglin also has "launch a ball and watch it bounce," but every relic changes *how the ball behaves*. Here the upgrades change *how much a bumper is worth*. Peglin wins on build identity.
- **vs Ball x Pit:** Ball x Pit makes you feel your build: more balls, weirder balls, the screen fills up. Moonshot's Multiball Splitter is the closest it gets, and I only saw it once.
- **vs Balatro / Luck be a Landlord:** the UI and readability of the cards is *Balatro-tier clear*. The math is the problem. Multiplier stuck at x1.0 is the opposite of Balatro's escalating-mult dopamine.
- **vs digital pinball (Pinball FX, Zaccaria):** obviously far less deep, but the bat-as-flipper aim mechanic is a genuinely fresh twist I haven't seen. The Upper Deck plus Home Run ramp is the first moment it felt like a "real table."

## 9. Verdict

**6/10 as it stands.** It's polished, readable, and has a legitimately original core verb. It's also too easy early, too passive between swings, and the roguelite layer is mostly "+X% on bumper Y."

- **$0.99?** Probably yes on mobile, because it's clearly well made. Wishlist on Steam? Yes. Would I share it? Not yet. There's no clip-worthy moment in the first 5 minutes except maybe the Home Run ramp.

**Top 3 things that would most move my score (ranked):**
1. **Make the pitching (and batting) the game.** Give the pitcher a varied arsenal (inside/outside, curveballs, change-ups, faster Closers as bosses) and tie upgrades to *how you hit*: pulled hits, opposite-field hits, perfect-timing "sweet spot" crits, hit streaks. Right now the most baseball part of the game is the least developed.
2. **Make builds escalate.** Fix the multiplier so it actually climbs. Give boosts real synergies (e.g. "every Glove catch +0.5× for this ball"). Let me overkill the target instead of yanking the ball. Stop offering machines I can't fit. Put machines in the shop. I want to see x8.0 and a five-digit hit by inning 3.
3. **Readability plus impact on the bat.** A bigger ball (especially on the Upper Deck), a bigger batter, hit-stop/flash/"CRACK" on contact, and new parts that look as chunky as the factory bumpers. Also tune early difficulty: 1-ball clears for C1–C4 means strikes don't matter yet.
